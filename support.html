ShotSyncCam Support

ShotSyncCam is a companion app for Wi-Fi body cameras. Currently supported hardware: VIEVU LE4.

Turn on camera Wi-Fi

ShotSyncCam connects over the camera’s own Wi-Fi network. If you do not see a camera network on your iPhone, the camera’s Wi-Fi may be off.

Important — used cameras and Wi-Fi:

  • New cameras usually ship with Wi-Fi On. You should see a network named like LE4-XXXXXX when the camera is powered on. No special cable is needed.
  • Used or former agency cameras may have had Wi-Fi turned Off by the previous owner in VIEVU’s desktop software. That saved setting is reapplied every time the camera starts, so the camera will not show a Wi-Fi network and the iPhone app cannot turn it on by itself (it can only connect after the network is already up).
  • To turn Wi-Fi on again you need one of the following:
    • The VIEVU USB-to-serial (FTDI) console cable, then the serial steps below (custom wifi on). This lasts only until the camera powers off; you must run it again after each power-on.
    • VIEVU’s own desktop software (VIEVU Solution / VERIPATROL with Solution Connector, or a VIEVU dock): open Settings → Cameras, set Wi-Fi to On, Save, then connect the camera so the setting is written. VIEVU discontinued that software in July 2025, so many users no longer have it.
  • Without the serial cable or that software, you cannot turn Wi-Fi on for a camera that was saved as Off.
  • Advanced: with the serial cable you can also make the camera turn Wi-Fi on by itself at every power-on. See Keep camera Wi-Fi on at every power-on.
  • If the camera will not stay powered on without being plugged in, the battery may be worn or flat. Charge it fully and try again.
  • Before buying a used LE4: ask whether its Wi-Fi network appears when the camera is powered on.

On a VIEVU LE4 you can turn Wi-Fi on for the current power session from the camera’s USB serial console:

  1. Connect the camera to a computer using the serial / FTDI leg of the camera’s USB Y-cable (the data or console connector, not a charge-only cable).
  2. Open a serial terminal at 115200 8N1 (115200 baud, 8 data bits, no parity, 1 stop bit). Examples: screen or picocom on macOS and Linux, or PuTTY on Windows.
  3. Press Enter until you see the cmd> prompt.
  4. Type custom wifi on and press Enter.
  5. Check that a camera network appears in your Wi-Fi list. The name is typically of the form LE4-XXXXXX. The broadcast name may differ from the serial printed on the camera label.
  6. To turn Wi-Fi off later, type custom wifi off at the same cmd> prompt and press Enter.

The Wi-Fi password is the factory default printed in your camera documentation.

The serial custom wifi on command does not change the camera’s saved Wi-Fi preference. If the network disappears after the camera powers off, run it again (or use VIEVU’s software to save Wi-Fi On, if you still have it).

Safety: Do not run other console commands unless you understand them. This is unofficial guidance, not from the camera manufacturer, and you use it at your own risk. ShotSyncCam is a third-party companion app and is not affiliated with or endorsed by VIEVU.

Advanced: keep camera Wi-Fi on at every power-on

For a camera whose saved Wi-Fi setting is Off, the steps below change the setting so the camera turns its Wi-Fi on by itself every time it starts, the way a new camera does. They have been tested on one VIEVU LE4. Treat them as experimental.

Requirements

  • The camera’s special USB-to-serial (FTDI) console cable: the serial / console leg of the camera’s USB Y-cable.
  • A computer with a serial terminal set to 115200 8N1, for example picocom -b 115200 /dev/ttyUSB0 on Linux or screen on macOS.
  • This cannot be done from the iPhone app, over the camera’s Wi-Fi, or with a normal USB cable. The change is made through the serial console, and changing camera settings over Wi-Fi did not save it.

Warning: This changes values in the camera’s memory with low-level console commands. It is unofficial, advanced, and done entirely at your own risk. A typing mistake can change the wrong setting or make the camera unusable. Keep the camera on its charger the whole time. Type one command at a time and wait for the cmd> prompt before typing the next. Stop if anything does not match what is described here.

  1. Connect the console cable, open the serial terminal, and press Enter until you see cmd>.
  2. Find the camera’s live settings by typing:
    search l 0xa0000000 +0x800000 0x10010101
    It prints a list of addresses, usually about eight. The addresses change every time the camera starts, so always search again after a restart.
  3. For each address H in the list, subtract 8 (hex) to get S, then check it with dump b S +0x20. Keep only the addresses whose two output lines read exactly:
    00 00 00 00 05 ff 00 00-01 01 01 10 00 00 00 00
    01 02 00 00 00 00 00 00-02 01 01 01 00 00 00 00
    In our test, four addresses matched. The others hold unrelated data, so do not change them. If none match, stop.
  4. For each matching S, add 1E (hex) to get the target byte, set it to 01, then check it:
    fill b S+0x1e +1 0x01
    dump b S+0x10 +0x10
    The check must read exactly 01 02 00 00 00 00 00 00-02 01 01 01 00 00 01 00. If any other value changed, stop and power the camera off and on before doing anything else. Changes in memory are lost at power-off unless the camera saves them.
    Example: if S is 0xa05df6d0, type fill b 0xa05df6ee +1 0x01, then check with dump b 0xa05df6e0 +0x10.
  5. Close the serial terminal. Turn the camera off normally with its power button. Do not unplug it or let the battery die: the camera saves its settings during a normal power-off. Wait 30 seconds, then turn it on.

Check it worked: without typing custom wifi on, wait about a minute after power-on. A network named like LE4-XXXXXX should appear in your Wi-Fi list. Power-cycle once more and check again to confirm it stays on.

Undo: repeat steps 1–5, but in step 4 type 0x00 instead of 0x01. The check line then ends in 00 00 00 00. Then turn the camera off normally.

Notes:

  • Writing the value straight to the camera’s flash storage does not work: the camera overwrites it with its in-memory settings at power-off. Changing a camera setting over Wi-Fi from a computer also did not save it.
  • Not yet confirmed: whether the setting survives a fully drained battery, a factory reset, or a firmware update. If Wi-Fi stops starting on its own, repeat the steps.
  • Do not use other console commands, especially ones that erase, write, or format storage. If the camera stops working normally, stop and contact us before trying anything else.

Choosing among several cameras

On the Connect screen, tap Connect to Camera Wi-Fi:

  • If more than one camera network is saved in the app, a pick list appears. Choose the network you want, or tap Add camera and enter its Wi-Fi name.
  • If exactly one camera network is saved, the app joins that network.
  • If none are saved yet, the app joins any matching camera network and then shows which name it joined.

Successfully joined camera names are remembered for next time. After you connect, the Connect screen details show the real camera network name. Use Reconnect to Home Wi-Fi when you are finished.

FAQ

Can't connect? First make sure the camera’s Wi-Fi is on (see Turn on camera Wi-Fi). Allow Location and Local Network access in Settings › ShotSyncCam so the app can read the current network name and talk to the camera. If you have more than one camera, use the pick list to choose the right network.

Where are my clips? In the app's Library, and in the Files app under On My iPhone › ShotSyncCam.

Camera time wrong? Open Status and tap Sync Clock.

Will other cameras work? Not yet; more camera profiles may be added.
